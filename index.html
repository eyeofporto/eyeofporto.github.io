01 · History

2000 years
open to the sky

Porto is not just beautiful — it's a living book. Roman roots, Baroque gold, and azulejo walls that tell stories most visitors walk past without reading.

🏛️ Cloisters paid entry
Sé Catedral
Ribeira · 12th century

The oldest cathedral in the city. From the staircase outside you get one of the finest views over the Ribeira. The cloisters inside are covered in blue and white azulejos that are worth every cent of the entry fee.

Local tip: Go before 10am — at that hour it's almost only residents passing by.
📚 Ticket discounted on purchase
Livraria Lello
Bairro das Letras · early 20th c.

One of the most beautiful bookshops in the world — a spiral staircase and magnificent stained glass that inspired J.K. Rowling. Go, but avoid weekend afternoons when it becomes impossible.

Local tip: Your entry ticket discounts off any book purchase — bring home something by a Portuguese author.
🚂 Free entry
São Bento Station
City centre · 20th century

20,000 azulejo tiles narrating the history of Portugal. A train station that is simultaneously a museum. Even if you catch no train at all, walk in — it's worth every second.

Local tip: Look up. Most people photograph the main hall and never notice the details of the ceiling.
⛪ Entry includes catacombs
Igreja de São Francisco
Ribeira · 14th–17th century

The most gilded interior you will ever see — 400 kg of Baroque gold. The catacombs beneath still have bones on display. Not for everyone, but absolutely unforgettable.

Local tip: Combine with the Sé visit — they're 5 minutes apart on foot.
02 · Gastronomy

Eat where
locals eat

Eating in Porto is an experience on its own. Forget the pretty-fronted restaurants on the Ribeira — go where the locals go.

🥪
Francesinha
Porto's iconic dish

The queen of Porto cuisine. Bread, mixed meats, ham, chouriço, melted cheese and a beer-tomato sauce poured on top. Often served with fries underneath. Heavy, indulgent, mandatory.

Where to eat: Café Santiago (Rua Passos Manuel) — queue outside, worth every minute. Avoid the tourist-facing ones in the historic centre.
🐟
Bacalhau à Gomes de Sá
Invented right here in Porto

Invented in Porto in the 19th century. Flaked salt cod with potatoes, onion, olive oil and boiled egg. Pure comfort food — the dish Porto people eat when they miss home.

Local tip: Look for small tascas in Miragaia or Bonfim — honest prices and family recipes.
☕
Pastel de Nata + Coffee
The right way to start the day

In Porto, coffee isn't a "bica" — ask for a cimbalino. That's the local name and it tells them you know. Pair it with a pastel de nata from any neighbourhood bakery.

Local tip: Padaria Ribeiro (Cedofeita) or any neighbourhood bakery — you don't need a famous one to get a great one.
🍷
Porto Wine
The caves of Gaia

Technically produced in Vila Nova de Gaia just across the river. The great houses — Sandeman, Graham's, Ramos Pinto — offer guided cave tours with tastings included. Absolutely worth it.

Local tip: Go at dusk — the light over the Douro at that hour with a glass in hand is very hard to beat.
03 · Viewpoints

The city
from above

Porto is a city of hills — which means spectacular views at every turn. Here are the ones that truly earn the climb.

👁️ Free
Serra do Pilar
Gaia · the best view of Porto

From the other side of the river, in Gaia, you get the most iconic view of Porto — the Ribeira, the bridges, the full width of the Douro. The best photo of Porto is taken outside Porto.

Local tip: Sunset here is magic. Bring a bottle of wine and sit on the convent steps.
🗼 Paid climb
Torre dos Clérigos
City centre · 360° view

225 granite steps and at the top you have Porto in all 360°. The symbol of the city, built in the 18th century. At dusk the sky turns orange over the rooftops and it's absolutely breathtaking.

Local tip: Not worth it in rain — the mist covers everything. Pick a clear day.
🦚 Free
Jardins do Palácio de Cristal
Massarelos · romantic

Magnificent gardens with views over the Douro and its mouth. Peacocks wander freely. Perfect for an unhurried walk, especially in the morning or late afternoon.

Local tip: The gardens hide fountains and secluded corners most tourists never find — explore beyond the main lawn.
🪜 Free
Escadas do Guindais
Batalha → Ribeira on foot

Walk down the Guindais steps — a steep descent with the Ribeira slowly opening up before you. You arrive right at the river's edge. Better than any cable car.

Local tip: Walk up from the bottom for the best photo angle — the Sé cathedral appears in the background.
04 · Markets

Where the city
has a pulse

Markets are where the real city lives. Forget plastic souvenirs — go to a market and see how Porto people buy, eat and exist.

🛒 Free entry
Mercado do Bolhão
City centre · traditional

The historic market par excellence — recently restored. Fruit, fish, flowers, cheeses, charcuterie. The stall ladies still call you "menino" and "amor". A human experience before it's a tourist one.

Local tip: Go in the morning before 1pm — that's when it's alive. In the afternoon it empties out.
🍽️ Free entry
Mercado Bom Sucesso
Boavista · gourmet

A renovated market with restaurants and local producers. More touristy than Bolhão but high quality. A great lunch option — choose between petiscos, sushi, burgers and much more.

Local tip: The bar at the back has Douro wines at honest prices and a quiet atmosphere.
🪙 Free entry
Feira da Vandoma
Saturday mornings

Every Saturday near the Sé, an antiques and collectibles fair takes over. Stamps, coins, old azulejos, postcards, vinyl records. A living archive of the city. Wonderfully photogenic.

Local tip: Ask the price before showing too much interest — if they see you love it, the price goes up.
🎨 Free entry
Rua de Cedofeita
Neighbourhood · local commerce

The most authentic commercial street in Porto. Independent bookshops, design stores, small cafés, artists' ateliers. No chains — everything local. This is where creative Porto lives.

Local tip: Enter the perpendicular side streets — the Cedofeita neighbourhood hides courtyards and murals that appear in no guidebook.
05 · Secrets

The Porto
nobody shows you

These are the places locals keep to themselves — that most tourists will never find. This is where Porto surprises.

🌿 Local spot
Pátio das Virtudes
Bairro das Virtudes · hidden

A secret miradouro in the form of a suspended garden, with views over the Douro. Hidden behind a narrow street, Porto locals come here in the late afternoon with a beer and sit on the stone steps. Unique atmosphere.

Local tip: The entrance looks like a normal street — no signage. Find "Rua das Virtudes" and follow the sound of conversation.
🏊 Local spot
Douro River Beaches
Upstream · off the tourist trail

Further up the Douro, outside the city, there are river beaches where Porto people swim in summer. Cold water, no crowds, no entrance fee. The nature the city doesn't show you.

Local tip: You'll need a car. Worth combining with a visit to one of the Douro Valley quintas.
🖼️ Mostly free
Rua de Miguel Bombarda
Contemporary art street

The heart of contemporary art in Porto. Galleries, ateliers, independent spaces. On the first Friday of each month, openings are public events with wine and the artists present.

Local tip: Go on an opening Friday — it's a genuinely Porto social and artistic experience, not a tourist event.
🐈 Local spot
Bairro do Barredo
Ribeira · the medieval labyrinth

Behind the tourist Ribeira there's a maze of medieval alleyways, hanging laundry, cats and centuries-old houses. The Porto that survived time. Go without a map and get deliberately lost.

Local tip: The streets are very steep and the ground is uneven. Comfortable shoes are non-negotiable.
06 · Nightlife

Porto after
dark

Porto at night has its own energy. It's not a city that sleeps early — but it's not just clubs either. There's something for everyone.

🎸 Evening
Fado in Porto
Ribeira / Baixa · late evening

Porto has its own fado — more rustic and melancholic than Lisbon's. In small venues with low lighting, the atmosphere is completely unlike a tourist performance. Look for places that require advance booking.

Where to go: Casa da Guitarra (R. Santa Catarina) or Café Candelabro — spaces with genuine soul and no outrageous minimum spend.
🍺 Night
Galerias de Paris
City centre · bar street

The bar district. Dozens of small bars, terraces, live music in the street. Porto warms up slowly — between 11pm and 1am is the peak. Start early and let the night unfold.

Local tip: Avoid the most prominent bars at the entrance — the best ones are on the interior streets, less obvious.
⚓ Night
Cais de Gaia at Night
Gaia waterfront · river views

The Gaia quayside at night has the illuminated rabelo boats, the lit bridges, and Porto's reflection in the water. A terrace here with a Porto Tónico is difficult to surpass.

Local tip: Porto Tónico is white Port wine with tonic water and lemon — the drink of the moment. Refreshing and genuinely local.
🎛️ Night
Maus Hábitos / Plano B
City centre · culture & music

Two of Porto's most emblematic cultural spaces. Concerts, exhibitions, DJs, performance art. Not just clubs — spaces with curation and identity. Porto's serious nocturnal cultural scene.

Local tip: Check the weekly programme — entry is sometimes free and unexpected, memorable things happen.
