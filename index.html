01 · History

2000 years
open to the sky

Porto is not just beautiful — it's a living book. Roman roots, Baroque gold, and azulejo walls that tell stories most visitors walk past without reading.

🏛
Sé Catedral
Ribeira · 12th century
Cloisters paid entry

The oldest cathedral in the city. From the staircase outside you get one of the finest views over the Ribeira. The cloisters inside are covered in blue and white azulejos worth every cent.

Local tip: Go before 10am — at that hour it's almost only residents passing by.
📍 Open in Maps
📚
Livraria Lello
Bairro das Letras · early 20th c.
Ticket discounted on purchase

One of the most beautiful bookshops in the world — a spiral staircase and magnificent stained glass that inspired J.K. Rowling. Go, but avoid weekend afternoons.

Local tip: Your entry ticket discounts off any book purchase — bring home something by a Portuguese author.
📍 Open in Maps
🚆
São Bento Station
City centre · 20th century
Free entry

20,000 azulejo tiles narrating the history of Portugal. A train station that is simultaneously a museum. Even if you catch no train at all, walk in.

Local tip: Look up. Most people photograph the main hall and never notice the details of the ceiling.
📍 Open in Maps
⛪
Igreja de São Francisco
Ribeira · 14th–17th century
Entry includes catacombs

The most gilded interior you will ever see — 400 kg of Baroque gold. The catacombs beneath still have bones on display. Not for everyone, but absolutely unforgettable.

Local tip: Combine with the Sé visit — 5 minutes apart on foot.
📍 Open in Maps
🏛
Igreja do Carmo
City centre · 18th century
Free entry

One of Porto's most photogenic facades — entirely covered in azulejos on the side wall. Standing between two churches that share a single wall, it's an architectural curiosity unlike anything else in the city.

Local tip: Best photographed from across the street in the late afternoon when the tiles glow.
📍 Open in Maps
🌉
Ponte Luís I
Ribeira ↔ Gaia · 1886
Free

The iron double-deck bridge that defines Porto's skyline, opened in 1886 and designed by an engineer from Gustave Eiffel's school. The upper deck carries the metro and pedestrians; the lower one cars and walkers. Crossing it on foot is one of the great free experiences in the city.

Local tip: Walk the upper deck towards Gaia at sunset — the view back over the Ribeira is the best free panorama in Porto.
📍 Open in Maps
🏛
Palácio da Bolsa
Ribeira · 19th century
Paid entry

The 19th-century former stock exchange, built by the city's Commercial Association on the ruins of a convent. Behind a sober granite facade hides extraordinary opulence — above all the Arab Room, a gilded Moorish-revival hall that took years to decorate. Visits are by guided tour only.

Local tip: Book the guided tour ahead in summer, and choose one in your own language — the Arab Room is the moment everyone waits for.

02 · Culture & Architecture

The city
reinventing itself

Porto has always mixed the ancient and the bold. These are the spaces where the city's creative identity lives today.

🎵
Casa da Música
Boavista · Rem Koolhaas
Tours available

One of the most remarkable concert halls in Europe — designed by Dutch architect Rem Koolhaas, it looks like a chunk of crystal dropped from the sky. Worth visiting for the architecture alone.

Local tip: Guided architectural tours are surprisingly affordable. The interiors are as extraordinary as the exterior.
📍 Open in Maps
🍷
World of Wine (WOW)
Gaia · cultural & wine district
Multiple experiences

A world-class cultural destination with seven museums covering wine, chocolate, cork, Portuguese textiles and more. Stunning views over the Douro from the terraces.

Local tip: Buy a combined ticket for the museums that interest you most, and spend the rest of the time on the terraces with a glass of wine.
📍 Open in Maps
⚽
Estádio do Dragão
Antas · FC Porto
Match tickets / tours

Home of FC Porto — one of Europe's great football clubs. A match here is electric. The stadium tour is also excellent, with access to the dressing rooms, pitch and trophy room.

Local tip: If you can, go to a game. The atmosphere in the Curva Sul is raw, loud, and completely authentic Porto.
📍 Open in Maps
🖼
Rua de Miguel Bombarda
Contemporary art street
Mostly free

The heart of contemporary art in Porto. Galleries, ateliers, independent spaces. On the first Friday of each month, openings are public events with wine and the artists present.

Local tip: Go on an opening Friday — genuinely Porto, not a tourist event.
📍 Open in Maps
🏛
Alfândega do Porto
Miragaia · 19th century
Multiple experiences

The vast 19th-century customs house on the Miragaia riverfront, designed when the river was Porto's working port. Today it's a congress and exhibition centre that also houses the Museum of Transport and Communications — and one of the city's best perspectives back onto the bridges.

Local tip: Check what's on before you go — the building shines most during a temporary exhibition or festival, when its huge halls open up.
📍 Open in Maps
🌉
The Bridges of the Douro
Six crossings · Porto and Gaia
Mostly free

Six bridges span the Douro between Porto and Gaia, each from a different age: the railway Dona Maria Pia (1877, from Eiffel's workshop) and the double-deck Dom Luís I (1886), the soaring Arrábida (1963), and the modern São João (1991), Freixo (1995) and Infante (2003). Together they tell the city's story in iron, stone and concrete — and a seventh is already on the way: the Ponte da Ferreirinha, a metro-and-pedestrian crossing named after Dona Antónia Ferreira, the great 19th-century Port wine matriarch, expected around 2028.

Local tip: The classic way to see them is the 'Six Bridges Cruise' — a short boat trip up and down the river. Or walk the upper deck of the Dom Luís I at sunset for the best free view of them all.

03 · Viewpoints

The city
from above

Porto is a city of hills — spectacular views at every turn. Here are the ones that truly earn the climb.

👁
Serra do Pilar
Gaia · best view of Porto
Free

Across the river in Gaia, this is the classic view of Porto — the Ribeira, the bridges and the full sweep of the Douro. But the round building behind you is a monument in its own right: a 16th-century Augustinian monastery whose church and cloister are both perfectly circular, unique in Portugal and UNESCO World Heritage since 1996. The terrace and its panorama are free; the church and cloister are a ticketed visit.

Local tip: Sunset here is magic. Bring wine and sit on the convent steps.
📍 Open in Maps
🗼
Torre dos Clérigos
City centre · 360° view
Paid climb

225 granite steps and at the top you have Porto in all 360°. The symbol of the city, built in the 18th century. At dusk the sky turns orange over the rooftops.

Local tip: Not worth it in rain — the mist covers everything. Pick a clear day.
📍 Open in Maps
🌳
Jardins do Palácio de Cristal
Massarelos · romantic
Free

Magnificent gardens with views over the Douro and its mouth. Peacocks wander freely. Perfect for an unhurried walk, especially in the morning or late afternoon.

Local tip: Explore beyond the main lawn — hidden fountains and secluded corners most tourists never find.
📍 Open in Maps
🪜
Escadas do Guindais
Batalha → Ribeira on foot
Free

Walk down the Guindais steps — a steep descent with the Ribeira slowly opening up before you. You arrive right at the river's edge. Better than any cable car.

Local tip: Walk up from the bottom for the best photo angle — the Sé appears in the background.
📍 Open in Maps
🌅
Jardim do Morro
Gaia · sunset viewpoint
Free

A hillside garden in Gaia from 1927, right beside the upper deck of the Dom Luís I Bridge and its own metro stop. It's the most famous sunset spot in Porto — locals gather on the grass with a drink to watch the city light up across the river.

Local tip: Take metro line D to Jardim do Morro, or walk across the bridge's upper deck. Arrive before sunset to get a spot on the grass.
📍 Open in Maps
🌇
Miradouro da Vitória
Vitória · free viewpoint
Free

A simple, free terrace in the old Vitória quarter with one of the widest views in the centre — the Sé, the tiled rooftops, the Douro and the Dom Luís I Bridge all in one frame. It sits on private land but a sign welcomes visitors. Almost no infrastructure, just the view.

Local tip: Come at sunset and bring your own drink — there's nothing sold here, which is exactly why locals love it.

04 · Secrets

The Porto
nobody shows you

These are the places locals keep to themselves — that most tourists will never find.

🔍
Pátio das Virtudes
Bairro das Virtudes · hidden
Local spot

A secret miradouro in the form of a suspended garden, with views over the Douro. Hidden behind a narrow street, locals come here in the late afternoon with a beer and sit on the stone steps.

Local tip: No signage. Find "Rua das Virtudes" and follow the sound of conversation.
📍 Open in Maps
🗺
Bairro do Barredo
Ribeira · the medieval labyrinth
Local spot

Behind the tourist Ribeira there's a maze of medieval alleyways, hanging laundry, cats and centuries-old houses. The Porto that survived time. Go without a map and get deliberately lost.

Local tip: Very steep streets and uneven ground. Comfortable shoes are non-negotiable.
📍 Open in Maps
🌄
Fontainhas
Bonfim · river balcony
Local spot

A balcony over the Douro at the southern edge of Bonfim, laid out as a public promenade back in 1796. From its cliff you see four bridges at once and across to the Serra do Pilar. Half-forgotten for years, it's the heart of one of Porto's most beloved São João street parties every June.

Local tip: Skip the crowded Ribeira viewpoints and come here at dusk — and if you're in town on São João night, this is where the real party is.

Where the city
has a pulse

Mercado do Bolhão
City centre · traditional
Free entry

The historic market par excellence — recently restored. Fruit, fish, flowers, cheeses, charcuterie. The stall ladies still call you "menino" and "amor". A human experience before it's a tourist one.

06 · Gastronomy

Eat where
locals eat

Eating in Porto is an experience on its own. Forget the pretty-fronted restaurants on the Ribeira — go where the locals go.

🥪
Francesinha
Porto's iconic dish

The queen of Porto cuisine. Bread, mixed meats, ham, chouriço, melted cheese and a beer-tomato sauce poured on top. Often served with fries underneath. Heavy, indulgent, mandatory.

Where to eat: Café Santiago (Rua Passos Manuel) — queue outside, worth every minute.
🐟
Bacalhau à Gomes de Sá
Invented right here in Porto

Invented in Porto in the 19th century. Flaked salt cod with potatoes, onion, olive oil and boiled egg. Pure comfort food — the dish Porto people eat when they miss home.

Local tip: Look for small tascas in Miragaia or Bonfim — honest prices and family recipes.
☕
Pastel de Nata + Coffee
The right way to start the day

In Porto, coffee isn't a "bica" — ask for a cimbalino. That's the local name and it tells them you know. Pair it with a pastel de nata from any neighbourhood bakery.

Local tip: Padaria Ribeiro (Cedofeita) or any neighbourhood bakery — no famous place needed.
🍷
Porto Wine
The caves of Gaia

Technically produced in Vila Nova de Gaia just across the river. Sandeman, Graham's, Ramos Pinto — guided cave tours with tastings included. Absolutely worth it.

Local tip: Go at dusk — the light over the Douro with a glass in hand is very hard to beat.
🥖
Bifanas
Street food · Porto classic

Marinated pork in a crusty roll, soaked in a garlicky sauce. Porto's ultimate street food — cheap, fast, and absolutely addictive. The kind of thing you eat standing at a counter and immediately want another.

Where to eat: Conga or Cervejaria Gazela — both Porto institutions that have been doing this for decades.

09 · Nature & Beaches

Sea, river
and green

Porto is minutes from the Atlantic Ocean, the Douro estuary and some of the greenest parks in Portugal. Most tourists never leave the historic centre — their loss.

🌳
Parque da Cidade
Foz · largest urban park in Portugal
Free

83 hectares of lakes, meadows, forest trails and open space — the largest urban park in Portugal, stretching all the way to the sea. Where Porto people go to breathe.

Local tip: Walk through to the beach at Foz do Douro — you exit straight onto the Atlantic. One of the best urban walks in the country.

Praias de Vila Nova de Gaia
Gaia · Atlantic beaches
Free

Miramar, Aguda, Granja — beautiful Atlantic beaches just south of Porto. Wilder and less crowded, with dramatic cliffs and traditional fishing villages. The Chapel of Senhor da Pedra at Miramar sits on a rock in the sea.

Local tip: Visit the chapel at low tide for the best views and photographs.

Matosinhos · surf & seafood

The beaches closest to Porto — easy by Metro. Matosinhos is a surf beach with the best fresh seafood restaurants in the region right behind the sand. Leça da Palmeira has a stunning sea pool by architect Álvaro Siza.

11 · Beyond Porto

Beyond
the walls

The district of Porto reaches far beyond the city. North along the coast and east into the river valleys lie towns worth half a day or more — each with its own history, food and rhythm. If you have the time, leave the centre behind.

🏛
Vila do Conde
Coast · 30 min by Metro
Metro · Line B

A handsome coastal town at the mouth of the River Ave, half an hour north on Metro Line B. Its great 18th-century Santa Clara aqueduct — once 999 arches and the second longest in Portugal — still marches across the fields towards the hilltop monastery. Add a working fishing harbour, long beaches and centuries-old convent sweets.

Local tip: On Metro Line B, get off at Santa Clara for the aqueduct, or Vila do Conde for the old town and the river.
📍 Open in Maps
🏛
Aqueduto de Santa Clara
Vila do Conde · 18th-century aqueduct
Free

Built between 1705 and 1714 to carry water from a spring in Terroso all the way to the Santa Clara convent, this is one of Portugal's great works of civil engineering — and said to have had 999 arches, the most of any aqueduct in the world. Around 5 km survive across the fields, the best-preserved stretch climbing towards the old convent. A National Monument since 1910.

Local tip: On Metro Line B, get off at Santa Clara — the arches rise right by the station, best photographed in late-afternoon light.
📍 Open in Maps
⛵
Nau Quinhentista
Vila do Conde · 16th-century ship
Small fee

A full-size replica of a 16th-century Portuguese nau, moored on the River Ave since 2007 and built in the local Samuel & Filhos shipyards using age-old techniques. Step aboard and you find the captain, pilot and crew recreated at their posts, with navigation instruments, cargo and an apothecary — a vivid window into life at sea during the Age of Discovery. Part of the Royal Customs House naval museum next door.

Local tip: Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am–6pm (last entry 5:15pm), closed Mondays. Combine it with the aqueduct and the old town for a half-day in Vila do Conde.
📍 Open in Maps
🌊
Póvoa de Varzim
Coast · end of Metro Line B
Metro · Line B

Right at the end of Metro Line B, this is a proper seaside resort with a deep fishing soul. Wide Atlantic beaches and a long promenade, a lively seafront, fresh fish everywhere, and one of Portugal's classic casinos. Look out for the 'siglas poveiras' — the symbols local fishing families once used in place of surnames.

Local tip: Pair it with Vila do Conde — they sit side by side, a stop or two apart, and make an easy day together.
📍 Open in Maps
🏰
Forte de Nossa Senhora da Conceição
Póvoa de Varzim · seafront fortress
Free

Póvoa's seafront landmark: a star-shaped fort with four bastions, built between 1701 and 1740 under kings Pedro II and João V to guard the fishing harbour from pirates. A small chapel from 1743 still sits inside. For most of the 20th century it was a customs-guard barracks; the town reopened it to the public in 2015, and today its parade ground and esplanades sit right beside the casino and the marina.

Local tip: Usually only the outside and the courtyard are open — pair the visit with a walk along the seafront and a plate of fresh fish at the harbour.
📍 Open in Maps
🌉
Amarante
Tâmega valley · ~1h east
By car · ~1h

An hour east, where the plain gives way to mountains, Amarante drapes itself over the green River Tâmega. Its postcard is the granite São Gonçalo bridge and the church and convent beside it, home to a museum of modernist painter Amadeo de Souza-Cardoso. The riverside cafés and famous convent sweets are reason enough to go.

Local tip: Easiest by car. Try the convent sweets at Confeitaria da Ponte — and ask the locals, with a smile, about the cheeky 'doces de São Gonçalo'.

⛪
Ponte e Igreja de São Gonçalo
Amarante · bridge and church
Free

Amarante's postcard: a three-arch granite bridge rebuilt in 1790 and the great church and convent of São Gonçalo rising beside it. The bridge is famous for its 14-day 'heroic defence' against Napoleon's retreating army in 1809 — cannonball scars still mark the church wall. Inside, this National Monument holds the tomb of São Gonçalo, the matchmaker saint, and the Amadeo de Souza-Cardoso museum.

Local tip: Touch the statue above the saint's tomb — tradition says the single will find a spouse within the year. Then try the cheeky 'doces de São Gonçalo' nearby.
📍 Open in Maps
⛰
Monte de São Félix
Laundos · hilltop viewpoint
By car · ~1h

The highest point of the Serra de Rates, 202 m above the coastal plain near Póvoa de Varzim. From the top — old windmills, a chapel and the striking Monument to the Emigrant — you get a true 360°: the Atlantic to the west, the green hills of the Minho to the east. A quiet detour few tourists ever make.

Local tip: Go by car and pair it with the beaches of Póvoa or Vila do Conde — and time it for late afternoon, when the light over the ocean is at its best.

12 · Getting Around

Moving through
the city

Porto is compact but hilly. Knowing which transport to use — and when — saves time, money and energy. Here's what a local uses.

🚇
Metro do Porto
6 lines · fast & affordable

The fastest way to cross the city. 6 colour-coded lines covering Porto, Gaia, Matosinhos, Maia and the airport. Uses the Andante card — buy one at any station and top it up. Single trips or daily passes available. Runs until around midnight.

Local tip: The Yellow Line (D) crosses the Dom Luís I Bridge at the upper level — one of the best views in the city, from inside a metro carriage, completely free.
🚌
STCP Bus Network
Urban buses · city-wide coverage

The bus network covers areas the Metro doesn't reach, including the city centre's narrow streets, the coast (Foz, Matosinhos), and residential neighbourhoods. Same Andante card as the Metro. Can be slower due to traffic but highly useful for shorter hops.

Local tip: Bus 500 follows the coastline from Matosinhos to Foz do Douro — scenic and practical at the same time.
🚆
CP Comboios (Train)
Regional trains · Douro & coast

CP regional trains depart from São Bento and Campanhã stations. The coastal line (Linha do Minho) reaches Viana do Castelo. The Douro Line is one of the most scenic train journeys in Europe — through vineyards and river gorges all the way to Pinhão.

Local tip: The Douro Line train from Porto to Pinhão is a half-day trip that most tourists miss entirely. Stunning, affordable, unforgettable.
🚲
Gira Bike Sharing
Electric bikes · flat routes

Porto's shared electric bike system. Great for the flatter areas — along the Douro riverfront, through Foz, or between Boavista and the coast. The hills of the historic centre make cycling tough, but e-bikes help enormously. App-based rental.

Local tip: The riverside cycle path from Ribeira to Foz do Douro is around 7km of flat, beautiful cycling. One of the best ways to spend a morning.
⛴️
River Ferry
Cross the Douro · scenic

Small passenger ferries cross the Douro between Porto and Vila Nova de Gaia. The most scenic is between Ribeira and the Gaia caves. Cheap, quick, and a completely different perspective from the water.

Local tip: Take the ferry instead of walking the bridge — you look up at the Dom Luis I Bridge from the water. Spectacular.
🚕
TVDE (Uber / Bolt)
Door to door · always available

TVDE platforms (Uber and Bolt) are widely used in Porto and highly reliable. For airport transfers, late nights, heavy luggage, or when the Metro doesn't go where you need — this is the answer. Book directly from the app, track your driver in real time, and pay automatically.

Local tip: Your driver might just have a guide to the city — ask them what they recommend. Locals always know more than guidebooks.
🚋
Historic Tram (Electrico)
Since 1872 · living heritage

Three historic lines still operate: Line 1 along the Douro, Line 18, and Line 22 through the city centre. The iconic yellow wooden carriages are slow, loud, and completely magical. Porto's tram network was the first in the Iberian Peninsula.

Local tip: Line 1 along the Douro from Infante to Foz — 30 minutes, a few euros, and a window into old Porto that no other transport can give you.
